# 1 · WATERMARK CONCEPTS (five)

## Universal specs (apply to all five)

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Export size | Height = **1.5–2% of the long edge**. On a 2000px export: ~30–40px cap height |
| Opacity — dark images | **12–18%** white `#FFFFFF` |
| Opacity — light images | **15–22%** black `#1A1A1A` |
| Shadow | 1px, 20% opacity, 120° — keeps it legible on mixed backgrounds |
| Font | Geometric sans, uppercase, **tracking +150 to +200**. Inter · Avenir Next · Montserrat |
| Never | Scripts (illegible at size) · black/bold weights (distracting) · opacity above 25% |

## CONCEPT 1 — THE QUIET MARK
*Gallery-minimal. One line, one hairline.*

- **Mark:** `@VATSALSHARMA.PHOTO`, Inter uppercase, tracking 150, 14% white
- **Detail:** a hairline rule 24px above the text, width matched to the text, 1px, same opacity
- **Placement:** bottom-right, 2.5% margin
- **Crop resistance:** ★☆☆☆☆ (corner = gone in two seconds)
- **Best for:** the Instagram feed, where hub accounts may repost you
- **Verdict:** safest, weakest. Use it where growth matters.

## CONCEPT 2 — THE FLYWAY GLYPH ★ *primary recommendation*
*Two-point mark. Three ascending strokes = a flock in migration.*

- **Mark A (top-left):** the glyph — three ascending 1.5px strokes, 28px tall, 12% opacity, 4% margin
- **Mark B (bottom-right):** the handle in Inter uppercase, tracking 150, 12%, 2.5% margin
- **Placement:** opposite corners, so cropping one leaves the other
- **Crop resistance:** ★★★★☆
- **Bonus:** the glyph stands alone as your **maker's chop** — blind-embossed on a print, or etched, with no text at all. That's a real fine-art convention
- **Verdict:** the strongest brand asset here. Ties to the Central Asian Flyway story. Use everywhere.

## CONCEPT 3 — THE PLATE MARK
*A museum wall label.*

- **Mark:** centred at the bottom — `Vatsal Sharma · Halcyon · Thol, 2026` in **Cormorant Garamond italic**, 12% opacity
- **On prints:** add the edition number, e.g. `3/25`
- **Placement:** centred bottom, 3% margin
- **Crop resistance:** ★★☆☆☆ (centred marks are usually cropped with the bottom edge)
- **Best for:** website, print files, exhibition prints — it makes the image read as an art object rather than social content
- **Verdict:** weakest protection, strongest sales psychology.

## CONCEPT 4 — THE EMBEDDED MARK
*Anti-crop by position, not by opacity.*

- **Mark:** the handle at 10% placed **inside the subject zone** — over blurred background *adjacent to* the bird, never over the bird
- **Detail:** tiny, 8–10% — visible on close inspection, invisible at scroll speed
- **Crop resistance:** ★★★★★ — cropping it out means cutting into the bird
- **Best for:** your ten best frames, the ones you'd actually sell
- **Verdict:** strongest protection of the five. Use on hero images only; too fussy for every post.

## CONCEPT 5 — THE MONOGRAM CHOP
*A seal, not a signature.*

- **Mark:** `VS` in a 1px circle, 32px diameter, Cormorant Garamond, 15%
- **Placement:** bottom-left, 2.5% margin
- **Crop resistance:** ★★☆☆☆
- **Best for:** print corners, the website footer, packaging and packing slips
- **Verdict:** good secondary mark. Pair it with Concept 2.

## RECOMMENDED COMBINATION
| Surface | Mark |
|---|---|
| Instagram feed | **Glyph** (both points) at 10–12% — or nothing, if a hub feature is likely |
| Portfolio website / print files | **Plate Mark** + **Monogram Chop** |
| Your ten best frames | **Embedded Mark** + **Glyph** |
| Every file, always | **IPTC metadata** (below) |

## THE PART THAT ACTUALLY PROTECTS YOU
A visible mark is **deterrence**. Metadata is **evidence**. Fill these in File Info on every export:
- **Creator:** Vatsal Sharma
- **Creator Contact:** your email / handle
- **Copyright Notice:** © [year] Vatsal Sharma. All rights reserved.
- **Rights Usage Terms:** written permission required
- Keep EXIF intact on exports; register anything you sell.

# 2 · PROFILE PHOTO (three concepts, #1 primary)

**The test:** it must read at **110px** in the grid and survive at **32px** in a comment thread.

**Universal specs:** export **320×320** and **640×640** · keep all content inside a **80%-diameter safe circle** (Instagram crops the corners) · background `#16232A` · subject `#F2EBE0` · **no stroke under 2px** · no collages · no busy scenes.

### #1 — BIRD EYE MACRO ★ *your pick, and mine*
- **Content:** extreme macro of a bird's eye, pupil dead centre, filling ~80% of the circle
- **Colour:** desaturated cool iris (`#7C8B84`-ish) against a dark slate field; one warm catchlight
- **Why it wins:** already circular, maximum contrast, unmistakably "bird photographer" at 32px with zero text
- **Build:** shoot a frame, apply your base grade, crop 1:1 tight on the eye, add a radial burn at the edges, export 320/640
- **Check:** zoom to 32px. If the pupil doesn't read as a pupil, crop tighter.

### #2 — "VS" MONOGRAM
- **Content:** `VS` centred, Cormorant Garamond or Inter, letter-spacing +200, in `#F2EBE0`
- **Optional:** 2px `#D9C7A7` ring at the outer edge
- **Why:** clean, premium, works as a watermark and a stamp
- **Weakness:** reads as "designer" or "studio", not "photographer"
- **Check:** at 32px, letterforms must not fill in. Increase tracking if they do.

### #3 — FLYWAY GLYPH
- **Content:** the three ascending strokes, 1.5px → **scale to 4px stroke for the avatar** (thin lines vanish at small size), centred, `#F2EBE0` on `#16232A`
- **Why:** matches the watermark and becomes a coherent identity system
- **Weakness:** abstract — a new viewer won't know it's birds
- **Check:** at 32px the three strokes must still be three strokes. If they merge, widen the gaps.

# 3 · THE MIXED-CROP SYSTEM (you chose mixed)

Mixed ratios look messy unless a rule assigns them. Here's the rule.

## Assign ratio by frame type — never by mood

| Frame type | Ratio | Export pixels |
|---|---|---|
| Tight portrait (bird fills 70–90%) | **4:5** | 2160 × 2700 |
| Environmental / animalscape (bird ~⅓ height) | **3:2** | 2700 × 1800 |
| Flight / action / panoramic | **16:9** | 2700 × 1519 |
| Detail, macro, feather study | **1:1** | 2160 × 2160 |
| Reels | **9:16** | 2160 × 3840 |
| Carousel **cover** | **always 4:5** | 2160 × 2700 |

**The one hard rule: every carousel cover is 4:5**, whatever the interior slides are. This is what keeps the grid from looking random, because covers are what people see.

## Three more rules that hold mixed together
1. **Two identical ratios never touch** in the grid — alternate portrait and landscape along each row.
2. **The checkerboard still applies:** no two adjacent posts share both the same ratio *and* the same background temperature.
3. **Constant elements across every ratio:** identical grade, identical grain (22), identical vignette (−10), no borders, no watermarks on the feed. The grade is the cohesion, not the crop.

## Why 4:5 still leads
A 4:5 post occupies roughly **25% more screen area** than a square on mobile. Since your strongest frames should get the most real estate, **give your best work the 4:5 slot** and let the environmental and detail frames take the smaller ratios. Mixed crops, deliberate hierarchy.

## Grid check before posting
Lay nine thumbnails at 25% zoom. If the eye doesn't travel cleanly across a row, swap two posts. Every time.

- Type never crosses the bird
- Watermark never exceeds 3.5% of the frame
- Two typefaces, never three
