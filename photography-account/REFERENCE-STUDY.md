## PHOTOGRAPHER 1 — BENCE MÁTÉ (Hungary) · *The Invisible Bird Photographer*

**Who:** Wildlife Photographer of the Year 2010 ("Marvel of Ants"), and the only person to have won both the Young and the senior title [1](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/wpy/gallery/2025-avian-tailgating).

**What he actually did:** In **2005 he pioneered the one-way glass hide technique**, which "revolutionised the practice of hide photography worldwide" [1](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/wpy/gallery/2025-avian-tailgating). He built 25+ purpose-built hides at drinking stations, nest sites and feeding areas — including dedicated hides for European Roller, Hoopoe, Bee-eater and Kingfisher — **specifically designed for controlled lighting and clean backgrounds** [2](https://natureslens.co.uk/photography-holiday/hungary/wildlife-photography-hides-of-hungary/).

**Style breakdown:**
- **Background:** engineered, not found. Clean, even, uncluttered — the background is designed before the bird arrives
- **Angle:** one-way glass at ground and water level → eye-level and below-eye-level views nobody else gets
- **Light:** controlled at the hide — consistent, soft, directional
- **Mood:** behaviour-driven, patient, naturalistic rather than stylised
- **Composition:** frame-filling subjects of species that are otherwise impossible to approach
- **Colour:** natural, restrained, honest
- **Method cost:** extreme. His 2025 WPY image took **27 days in a hide**, with two synchronised cameras firing at 20 fps on remote release [1](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/wpy/gallery/2025-avian-tailgating)

**The lesson for you:** the background is a *decision made before the shutter*, not a problem solved in Lightroom. Máté engineers it. Most people clone it.

## PHOTOGRAPHER 2 — TIM FLACH (London) · *The Studio Purist*

**Who:** London-based animal photographer, "best known for his highly conceptual portraits of animals"; his work is "a departure from traditional wildlife photography" [3](https://timflach.com/work/more-than-human/).

**What he actually does:** Bird portraits **set on plain backdrops à la studio portraits**, deliberately removing the habitat — in the tradition of ornithological painters **John Gould and John James Audubon** [4](https://www.canon-europe.com/pro/stories/tim-flach-bird-photography/) [5](https://www.thisiscolossal.com/2019/11/tim-flach-birds/). He built a **mobile aviary** with a **turntable** so the bird rotates and he controls the light [4](https://www.canon-europe.com/pro/stories/tim-flach-bird-photography/).

**Style breakdown:**
- **Background:** total removal. Plain black or white, seamless
- **Light:** stylised studio lighting, plus daylight for larger birds — "I think daylight is really beautiful. So it's nice to have the mix" [4](https://www.canon-europe.com/pro/stories/tim-flach-bird-photography/)
- **Detail:** extreme. Shot on a **Canon EOS 5DS** (50.6MP) with a **100mm f/2.8L Macro**, at **f/18–f/20, 1/200 sec, ISO 100–200** [4](https://www.canon-europe.com/pro/stories/tim-flach-bird-photography/) — stopped right down for depth, on a tripod, with a static subject
- **Colour:** saturated but controlled; the bird is the entire frame
- **Mood:** fine-art, specimen-like, gallery. "Scientific precision" and "natural history illustration" [3](https://timflach.com/work/more-than-human/)
- **Composition:** head-and-shoulders portraits; personality and character over habitat

**The lesson for you:** total removal of context is a legitimate high-art position — and it is the exact opposite of what almost everyone on Instagram does. Also note: **f/18**. He trades light for depth because his subject is static and his camera is locked down.

## PHOTOGRAPHER 3 — MARSEL VAN OOSTEN (Netherlands) · *The Art Director*

**Who:** 15–16 years as an **art director and graphic designer** before turning full-time photographer; Wildlife Photographer of the Year and Travel Photographer of the Year [6](https://www.eizoglobal.com/solutions/casestudies/marsel-van-oosten/index.html) [7](https://guragear.com/blogs/news/a-conversation-with-marsel-van-oosten).

**What he actually does:** "In my work I try to simplify, to get rid of the extraneous: **simplicity is the ultimate sophistication**." His images are known for a "graphic approach to composition" — clean, unambiguous, strong shapes, clear visual hierarchy, and a sense of scale [6](https://www.eizoglobal.com/solutions/casestudies/marsel-van-oosten/index.html) [8](https://voiceoftheeyes.com/pages/photographer/marsel-van-oosten).

**Style breakdown:**
- **Composition:** graphic design applied to nature. **Dead trees preferred over live ones because they're more graphic** [7](https://guragear.com/blogs/news/a-conversation-with-marsel-van-oosten)
- **Subject size:** deliberately small. He favours large subjects *because* "the larger your subject, the less focal length you need, so the more landscape you can show" — the animal sits inside a landscape, not against a blurred wall of bokeh [7](https://guragear.com/blogs/news/a-conversation-with-marsel-van-oosten)
- **Workflow detail that matters:** he does most of his post-processing on images **resized to ~1,600 pixels wide**, working at 100% zoom [6](https://www.eizoglobal.com/solutions/casestudies/marsel-van-oosten/index.html). He edits for the size the image will actually be seen at.
- **Colour:** considered, ordered, high-clarity. "Overall look or setting is more important to me than the actual subject"
- **Mood:** serene, designed, monumental

**The lesson for you:** two things. (1) **Subject small in frame** is a legitimate and underserved composition — it's also what sells as wall art. (2) **Edit at the size it will be seen.** He judges at 1600px; you should be judging at Instagram grid size.

## THE GENRE BASELINE (what most people actually ship)

A representative "moody bird" preset circulating in 2026, verbatim [9](https://presetpedia.com/bird-photography-lightroom-presets/):

```
Exposure −0.20 | Contrast +15 | Highlights −25 | Shadows +25 | Whites −5 | Blacks −20
Temp −5 | Tint +3 | Vibrance +5 | Saturation −5
Clarity +15 | Dehaze +20 | Grain +25 | Vignette −25
Sharpening +25 | Chroma Noise +30 | slight S-curve
```

Note **Dehaze +20 and Vignette −25** — that combination is the sound of the genre. It's also the thing to avoid if you want to look different.

**2026 trend signals worth aligning with** [10](https://www.viewbug.com/blog/top-100-photography-trends-for-2026-whats-popular-whats-next-how-photographers-are-winning-on-viewbug):
- **Film-inspired colour grading (Kodak/Portra-style)** — rising
- **Grain as a deliberate texture choice, not an accident** — rising
- **"One colour" photo series** (colour discipline) — rising
- **Monochrome edits with one accent colour** — rising
- **Soft matte look** (controlled fade, not washed out) — rising
- **Crisp true-to-life colour, no heavy presets** — rising
- **Bird photography with clean backgrounds** — listed as a 2026 trend in its own right
- **Location ethics in nature photography · responsible wildlife shooting** — rising

## COMPARISON

### What all three share
1. **The background is a deliberate decision**, made before the shutter (Máté builds it, Flach removes it, van Oosten simplifies it)
2. **Consistency of method over variety of subject** — each is recognisable from a single frame
3. **Patience as the actual technique** — 27 days in a hide; a turntable; a graphic designer's edit
4. **Restraint in colour** — none of the three is chasing saturation
5. **A clear position on what to exclude**

### What each does uniquely
| | Máté | Flach | van Oosten |
|---|---|---|---|
| Background | Engineered in-camera | Removed entirely | Simplified to shapes |
| Subject size | Frame-filling | Head-and-shoulders | Small, inside landscape |
| Light | Controlled at the hide | Studio, f/18 | Natural, ordered |
| Colour | Natural, honest | Saturated, isolated | Considered, clean |
| Context | Full habitat | Zero habitat | Habitat as design |

### What nobody among them is doing — your gaps
1. **Temperature separation.** None of the three uses colour *temperature* to separate subject from background. Máté uses light, Flach uses removal, van Oosten uses geometry. The warm-bird/cool-habitat differential is unclaimed at this level.
2. **Place as brand.** All three are famous for *method*, none for *place*. Nobody owns Gujarat. Nobody owns the Central Asian Flyway as an identity.
3. **Seasonal grade.** All three are deliberately consistent year-round. A grade that breathes with migration season is nobody's signature.
4. **Editing for social scale.** Van Oosten edits at 1600px because he thinks about viewing size. Almost nobody edits *for the grid* while also producing a print file.
5. **Sound.** None of them. Ambient audio paired with stills is wide open.
