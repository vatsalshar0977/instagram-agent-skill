## 2. WHY THE TEACHING FORMULAS DON'T APPLY (and what replaces them)

The bird-niche swipe file says the top outlier is **#10 If This, Then Watch at 74x** — "If your bird photos look soft, this one setting fixes it." That's a *teaching* hook. It needs you talking, pointing at a menu, promising a fix. Wrong clothes for you.

I ran the portfolio-style hooks through `hookscore.py` and they all came back OK-to-WEAK (top score 56.0):

```
56.0 OK    Everyone photographs the bird. Nobody photographs the light.
56.0 OK    This is not a zoo. This bird chose to land here.
53.4 OK    I took four hundred frames of this kingfisher. One was sharp.
50.6 OK    Six hours in a hide for one frame. Sound on.
47.6 WEAK  Shot from a hide I built for two thousand rupees.
43.4 WEAK  Sound on. Dawn chorus, five forty seven am, Gujarat.
```

**That low score is the tool being wrong, not the hooks being wrong.** `hookscore.py` rewards: direct address ("you"), front-loaded claims, spoken stakes, hard numbers. A silent art Reel has none of those — its hook is a **picture**, and the text is atmosphere, not a promise.

So: **stop scoring your hooks with hookscore.py. Score your first frame.**

### The Frame-1 Test (use this instead)

Your Reel's first 0.5 seconds must pass all six. No words required.

- [ ] **Something moves.** Wing, water drop, head turn, grass in wind. Motion in frame 1 is the scroll-stopper. A static photo as frame 1 = scroll.
- [ ] **One clear subject.** If the eye has to search, it leaves.
- [ ] **The eye is visible and sharp.** Portraits work because of eyes. Birds are no different.
- [ ] **Clean negative space** for your text — sky, water, dark background, blurred bokeh.
- [ ] **Fills the frame vertically.** 9:16, no letterboxing, no borders, no watermark.
- [ ] **Sound exists.** Ambient audio — wings, water, wind, the shutter itself. "Sound on" is your only caption cue.

**If your frame 1 fails the motion test, open on 0.5s of the raw moment and cut to the still.** That's the whole trick.

Text on a portfolio Reel: short, lowercase or caps, no claims, no arrows, no "wait for it". Examples:
`4 HOURS · 1 FRAME` · `SOUND ON` · `5:47 AM, THOL` · `SHE CAME BACK AT DUSK` · `ONE LENS. ONE MORNING.`

## 3. THE SILENT BUILD — 3 to 4 weeks, do not post yet

A portfolio account launches once, with a full grid. Launching with three so-so frames and a "new account!" caption is how art accounts die. You are new to birds; your first outings will produce reference shots, not portfolio shots. That's fine — it's the cost of entry, and nobody sees it.

**Weeks 1–4: shoot, learn, post nothing.**

What you're learning in that time (birds, not cameras — you already know cameras):

1. **Identification.** Merlin (free, Cornell) for ID by photo/sound, eBird for what's being seen where, right now, within 20 km of you. Log every species you photograph.
2. **Prediction.** Visit **one patch repeatedly** — same lake edge, same tree line. Learn where they perch, what time they feed, how close they let you get. Prediction, not luck, is what makes bird photographs [1](https://www.debbiephotos.com/bird-photography-for-beginners/).
3. **Pre-flight signals.** A bird about to move tells you: thin neck, head-bobbing, alert posture, a shift of weight, a look up. Watch for takeoff cues [2](https://petapixel.com/2025/09/03/the-ultimate-beginners-guide-to-bird-photography/).
4. **Light discipline.** For art, shoot in the first 90 minutes after sunrise and the last 90 before sunset. Midday frames are record shots, not portfolio shots.
5. **Ethics.** Bird welfare above the photograph. No playback, no baiting, no flushing, no nest disturbance, never post exact locations of nesting rarities [2](https://petapixel.com/2025/09/03/the-ultimate-beginners-guide-to-bird-photography/). Ethics is also a trust asset for a print buyer.

**Your local patches:**
- **Indroda Nature Park, Gandhinagar** — ~400 ha, two sections; locally rated the best terrestrial birding near you [3](https://www.explorewithecokats.com/birdwatching-destinations-around-ahmedabad/)
- **Thol Lake Bird Sanctuary** (~40 km, Mehsana) — Ramsar site, 320+ species, peak **November–March**: flamingos, pelicans, ducks, herons, storks; Sarus Crane in the outlying fields; also Brahminy Starling, Green Bee-eater, Black-rumped Flameback, Spotted Owlet, Shikra [4](https://www.theindia.co.in/places/thol-bird-sanctuary)
- **Nalsarovar** (~64 km) — 200+ species, boat access, winter migrants
- **Vadla Lake** (near Nalsarovar) — walk-around dam birding, no boat needed

**Season note:** it's late September. Migration builds from October and peaks **Nov–Feb**. Your silent-build weeks line up perfectly with the season arriving. Be ready to shoot hard in November.

**Target for launch day:** 9 frames you'd print. Not 9 birds — 9 *frames*. Same edit, same mood.

## 4. THE LAUNCH

**Grid of 9, posted over 3 days or all at once.** One look, one grade, one ratio.

- **Ratio:** 4:5 portrait for feed (more screen area than square), 9:16 for Reels covers.
- **One preset, applied once, then adjusted per frame.** Consistency reads as authorship.
- **No watermark on the image.** Put your handle in the caption. A watermark on a gallery feed is the fastest way to look amateur and it kills reposts by hub accounts — which is your main growth lever (Section 7).
- **Cover grid:** if you want the 3-across title look, keep the text to 4 words max in the top half.

**Launch sequence:**
1. **Post 1 (Reel):** your single best moment — Format A below. This is the post that reaches non-followers.
2. **Posts 2–7:** six stills, one per post, spaced over 2 weeks.
3. **Post 8 (Carousel):** "One morning at [place]" — 5–8 frames. This is your save magnet.
4. **Post 9 (Reel):** the cinematic one — Format C. Establishes mood.

Then settle into **2–3 posts a week**: 1 Reel (reach) + 1 still (identity) + 1 carousel every other week (saves).

## 5. THE FOUR FORMATS (this is your whole content system)

### FORMAT A — The Single Frame (60% of posts) · 8–12 sec
**Structure:** 0.5s of the live moment → the still, slow push-in → hold on the eye → fade to black.
**Text:** one line. `4 HOURS · 1 FRAME` or `SOUND ON`.
**Audio:** ambient — wings, water, wind, the shutter. No music, or very low instrumental.
**Why:** it's a photograph that behaves like a Reel. Reach comes from Reels, identity comes from stills; this is both.

### FORMAT B — The Sequence (20%) · 10–15 sec
**Structure:** the actual burst, unedited order, 20 fps playback — bird lands, turns, takes off. Cut hard on the wingbeat.
**Text:** `1/2000 · [your lens] · THOL, 6:12 AM` (EXIF as aesthetic, not as teaching).
**How to get it:** **RAW burst mode with 0.5s pre-capture** on the R8 — half-press to start buffering, and when the moment happens you get the frames from *before* you fully pressed. This is the single most useful feature for takeoff and dive shots on your body [5](https://fstoppers.com/reviews/review-canon-eos-r8-mirrorless-full-frame-camera-630003).
**Why:** sequences prove you were there. They also get the most "how did you get this?" comments — which you answer in one line, not a tutorial.

### FORMAT C — The Wait (10%) · 20–30 sec
**Structure:** cinematic B-roll — hide going up in the dark, breath in cold air, empty branch, sun arriving, nothing happening, then the moment, then the still.
**Text:** minimal. Maybe just `SOUND ON`.
**Audio:** only ambient. Dawn chorus is the best sound you have.
**Why:** this is the piece that makes people follow rather than like. It's also the only place you show yourself.

### FORMAT D — The Series Carousel (10%) · saves
**Structure:** 5–8 frames, one morning or one species. Cover = strongest frame + 4 words.
**Caption:** one short paragraph about the morning.
**Why:** carousels are the save engine, and saves signal quality to the algorithm harder than likes do.

## 6. R8 FIELD SETTINGS FOR PRINT-QUALITY BIRDS

You know how to run a camera. These are the R8-specific decisions that matter for birds, and the traps in this body.

### Baseline (perched birds)
- **Mode:** Manual + Auto ISO. Shutter and aperture yours, exposure the camera's.
- **Shutter:** 1/1000 perched minimum; 1/2000 for anything moving; 1/3200+ for small fast birds in flight.
- **Aperture:** widest your lens gives, but stop down 1/3–2/3 stop if you need the sharpness or the depth of field to hold the whole bird.
- **ISO:** Auto, cap **6400** (12800 only in pre-dawn desperation). R8 files clean up fine at 6400.
- **AF:** Servo AF · Subject detect: **Animals** · Eye detect **ON**.
- **AF area:** **Spot AF** for perched birds through branches — whole-area tracking grabs the branch in front of the bird. Flexible Zone AF 1 for flight.
- **Drive:** high-speed continuous, **20 fps** as the default.
- **File:** **RAW** for anything you might print; CRAW for volume shooting.

### The R8 traps (specific to this body)
- **No IBIS.** Stabilisation comes from the lens only. Keep the shutter up, use IS, lean on something, or use a monopod/hide. Don't hand-hold at 1/250 because your old body could [6](https://lensandshutter.com/reviews/canon-r8-review/).
- **Rolling shutter at 40 fps.** 40 fps electronic is great for takeoff sequences, but fast wingbeats can skew. For clean portfolio frames use 20 fps (or electronic first-curtain at 6 fps) [6](https://lensandshutter.com/reviews/canon-r8-review/). **RAW burst mode: 30 fps with 0.5s pre-capture** — best tool on this camera for the moment you can't anticipate [5](https://fstoppers.com/reviews/review-canon-eos-r8-mirrorless-full-frame-camera-630003).
- **Single UHS-II SD slot.** No backup. Download and back up **every night**; format in camera, never in computer. One card failure in a portfolio workflow is unrecoverable [7](https://www.apcwildlife.com/blog/canon-r8-review-wildlife-photography).
- **LP-E17 battery, ~220 shots through the EVF.** Carry two spares. Cold mornings and 20 fps bursts drain it faster than you expect [6](https://lensandshutter.com/reviews/canon-r8-review/).
- **Silent shutter / electronic shutter mode** for skittish species — no shutter noise, no vibration.

### Workflow discipline
- Cull on a calibrated screen, not the back of the camera.
- Rate in three passes: sharp → keeper → portfolio.
- Anything you post gets a final check at 100% on the eye. If the eye isn't sharp, it isn't art, it's a record shot.

## 7. GROWTH — how a portfolio account actually grows

You don't have teaching hooks to pull reach with. You have four levers. Use them in this order.

**1. Hub features (biggest lever by far).**
Bird feature accounts repost photos and their audience follows the photographer. From the research: **@best_birds_of_ig** (402K), **@bestbirdshots** (311K), **@birdfreaks** (850K), **@birdsonearth** (520K). Also worth: @birdwatchingmag, @audubonsociety-style orgs, India-specific birding pages.

**Start local, it converts faster: `@birds.of.gujarat`** — a Gujarat bird photographic database, 545 species, founded by @dr.anand.patel and @escape_into_the_wild [8](https://birdsofgujarat.co.in/aboutus.php). Submitting Gujarat frames to them is on-mission rather than self-promotion, and their audience is local birders — your first real print buyers. Add them, plus both founders, to your daily 20.
- Tag them in the caption and the first comment. Many require a hashtag or a tag in the image.
- **Never watermark** — watermarks are why feature accounts skip otherwise good photos.
- A single feature from a 400K hub can do more than a month of your own posting.

**2. Saves via carousels.** Saves are the strongest quality signal and they reach non-followers. Post one series carousel every other week.

**3. Local discovery.** Geotag every post (Thol, Nalsarovar, Indroda). Gujarat birders search locations. Use `#birdsofgujarat` `#birdphotographyindia` `#gujaratbirding`.

**4. Outside Instagram.** Post select frames to Reddit r/birdphotography and r/birding, Flickr/500px groups, and local Gujarat birding WhatsApp/Facebook groups. Drive traffic *to* the account, not just to the photo.

**Collabs:** once you have 20 frames, use Instagram's collab feature with a local birding group or a conservation org. Collab posts show to both audiences.

**What to ignore:** follow-unfollow, engagement pods, giveaway loops, "comment for comment". They add followers who will never buy a print.

## 8. THE PRINT FUNNEL

Don't open the shop on day one. Build it in this order.

1. **Bio line:** `Bird photography · Gujarat · Prints available` — signals intent without a hard sell.
2. **Single link:** one page that lists 5–8 prints with sizes and prices. Not a generic homepage. Print-on-demand (Printful/Printlr) or local lab + your own checkout.
3. **Highlight: `PRINTS`** — price list, paper types, size guide, packing photo, how to order (DM or link).
4. **Occasional "available" post** — 1 in 5 posts may end with `Prints of this frame: link in bio`. Not every post.
5. **Email list** from the first inquiry. Ten buyers who hear from you beat a thousand followers who don't.
6. **Offline:** Ahmedabad/Gandhinagar art spaces, nature societies, and birding festivals. Local exhibitions convert print buyers far better than DMs.

**Price anchor:** start with an open or small limited edition per frame, note the edition size if limited, and always show the print in a room (framed on a wall photo) — people can't visualise scale.

## 9. CAPTIONS AND HASHTAGS

**Caption shape for a portfolio account** — atmosphere, then fact, then optional availability:

```
She came back to the same dead branch for four mornings. On the fifth, the light finally arrived with her.

White-throated Kingfisher · Thol Lake, Gujarat · 6:12 am
Canon R8 · [your lens] · 1/2000 · f/[x] · ISO [y]

Prints of this frame — link in bio.
```

Short. No emoji walls. No "📸📸📸". No "follow for more". The photograph is the hook; the caption is a whisper.

**Hashtags: 5, plus feature tags.**
`#birdphotography` `#birdsofinstagram` `#birdphotographyindia` `#birdsofgujarat` `#wildlifephotography`
Then tag the feature accounts you want to be reposted by.
Avoid `#bird` `#nature` `#photooftheday` — tens of millions of posts, zero buyer intent.

## 11. MEASUREMENT (portfolio edition)

- **Saves per reach** — your real quality metric. Higher saves = frames people want to own.
- **Profile visits → follows** — above ~20% means the grid is converting.
- **Reach from non-followers** on Reels — this is the only growth number that matters early.
- **Feature-account reposts** — count them; each is a step change.
- **Print inquiries per month** — the only number that pays.

After your first 10 posts, run `/ig-audit` to find your own outlier multiple. Views alone mean nothing until you have a median to compare against.
